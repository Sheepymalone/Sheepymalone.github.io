I'm a PhD student in **Biophysics** at the **University of Michigan**, working at the
interface of optical physics and cell biology. My background is in building and
applying single-molecule and interferometric imaging systems, and in the
computational pipelines needed to get quantitative answers out of them.

Before Michigan I completed a B.Eng. in Bioengineering at **Nanyang Technological
University** in Singapore, where I built interferometric scattering (iSCAT)
microscopes in the Tong Ling Lab to track membrane dynamics in live cells, and
developed a low-cost nanoparticle-based detection assay at IDMxS. I also spent a
summer in the Huang Lab at Purdue applying transformer architectures to
single-molecule localization microscopy.

## Research interests

- Single-molecule fluorescence and super-resolution microscopy
- Interferometric and label-free imaging (iSCAT)
- Quantitative live-cell imaging of cellular dynamics and structure
- Computational image analysis: segmentation, particle tracking, deep learning
